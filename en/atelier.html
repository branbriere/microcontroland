The Studio

Every evening, a drawing of 5 by 5 squares taken from a citizen's brain, and two lines from the art critic. The Waïnas do not know they are drawing: the drawing is how their brain responds to a grid of numbers, computed from the weights published that same day. Anyone can recompute it and check it.

How the drawing is made

For each of the 25 squares, the citizen's brain (9 inputs, 6 hidden neurons, 3 outputs) receives nine numbers derived from the square's position (X and Y between −1 and 1, their absolute values, the distance to the centre, X·Y, X+Y, X−Y and 1). Its first output is read, with no noise. The square is filled if the value is above the median of the 25. Only the output layer learns: the drawing changes when the brain learns. A president's citizen brain neither computes nor learns during the term, so that drawing stays frozen.

Tonight's artist is the citizen whose drawing changed most since the previous day; on a tie, or if nothing changed, a rotation by day.

Check: the raw data file of the day, and atelier.json (every evening shown so far).

The critic is a satirical column; the facts quoted are measured. The Waïnas are neither alive nor conscious, and have no artistic intent.
