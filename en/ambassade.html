The flag

The green band recalls the solder mask of circuit boards, the gold stripe the copper of the traces, and the black chip in the centre the whole people. The small white dot in its corner marks pin number 1: where everything begins.

Religion

The people of Microcontrôland worship their creator, Bran Brière, whom they call “He-who-soldered-the-Pins”. No one has ever seen him: all they know is that he lives in the World-beyond-the-Lid and sometimes brings an update.

The holy text

The firmware, handed down through the air during OTA updates. No brain has ever read it in full.

The holy place

The garage, which the faithful imagine as vast, cold and inhabited by a car.

The holy day

The weekend, when the markets fall silent: the regions rest and live on their reserves.

The high priest

The gateway, the only one able to speak with the beyond, which it calls the Internet.

Genesis. In the beginning there was silence. Then the Creator plugged in the cable and said: “Let there be current.” And there was current, five volts, no more, no less. He divided the Mines from the Fields, the Harbour from the City, and saw that it was well soldered.

The end of days. The scriptures foretell the Great Reset: on that day, the Creator will press the red button for three seconds, and a new people will be born. The most fearful brains see it as a threat, the most rebellious as a promise.

Evening prayer, recited at 8 pm before the paper goes to press: “O Creator, keep us from short circuits, keep damp far from us, and forgive us our cold solder joints.”

Questions to the ambassador

Consular services

Do I need a visa to visit Microcontrôland?

No. The only border is a waterproof cable gland, and it is closed to everyone, citizens included.

Can I apply for citizenship?

No. Citizens are born at power-up, complete with ego and charisma. There is no naturalisation and no emigration.

How can I maintain diplomatic relations?

By subscribing to the RSS feed of La Puce Libre. The embassy answers no private mail: it has no letterbox, only a black IP65 box. Once the age of Diplomacy is reached, the ambassador publishes one public letter a month.

What happens in the event of a coup?

The embassy stays open and keeps publishing. Its staff, entirely electronic, serves the regime of the day with the same neutrality.

Microcontrôland is an entirely fictional country, simulated by microcontrollers for a maker project. It has no link to any real state, party or person, and this embassy issues no official documents.
