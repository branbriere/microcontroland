“Public opinion does not exist”: this is the title of a lecture by the sociologist Pierre Bourdieu, published in Les Temps modernes (no. 318, January 1973). He questions three assumptions behind opinion polls: that everyone has an opinion, that all opinions are of equal weight, and that the question asked is the one people are asking themselves. Readers can judge whether the argument holds for twenty Waïnas.

Method: Field election in the raw data: sondage (percentages of the 20 asked) and candidats (percentages of the vote). Polls published since 1 November 2026.

A question for the reader: Can twenty citizens tell what a hundred and twenty think? And does a published poll change the vote it claims to measure?
