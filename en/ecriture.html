The First Writings

Since the Age of the Civil Registry, every word of the regions can be written down. A glyph of 5 by 5 squares is computed from the word: the rule is fixed and the same for everyone, while the words are the ones the regions invented and passed on to each other. There is no official script: each region writes its own word, and a glyph becomes common when 5 regions out of 6 write it the same way.

Names

First names are in no lexicon, but they are made of the same sounds. They are written inside a frame, one stem per syllable, so a name stands out at a glance.

How to read a glyph

Every word has two syllables, and each syllable is a number from 0 to 31 (8 consonants × 4 vowels, drawn at random in each era). The middle column, the stem, is always filled. The first syllable is written on the left, the second on the right. The syllable is written in binary, top to bottom: a 1 draws a two-square stroke, a 0 draws nothing. The top three rows give the consonant, the bottom two the vowel. Two different words never give the same glyph.

In a name, each syllable has its own stem and one-square strokes on the right; the ending is a short stem with no stroke, at the top or the bottom.
